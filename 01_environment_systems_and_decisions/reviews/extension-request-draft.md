## On stating why the notification was missed

Keep it, and keep it specific. Two factors combined, and both are factual:

1. The address is not monitored daily.
2. **The subject line changed format.** Springer's migration to SNAPP sends journal-branded
   notifications — *"Environment Systems and Decisions: Decision on your manuscript"* — rather than the
   older *"Springer Nature"* heading. A message that does not match the expected pattern is exactly the
   kind that slips past inbox rules and visual scanning.

Point 2 matters because it is a **concrete, verifiable explanation rather than a plea**. The Editorial
Office is aware of the platform change and has heard this before. Mentioning it is not blame-shifting
as long as the wording stays neutral — the draft says the message "was not picked up," not that
Springer caused the delay.

The request is 35 minutes past a *recommended* date, so there is nothing to excuse. State the reason
once, then pivot to the work in progress: **honest, unapologetic, forward-looking**. Do not apologise
repeatedly — that would make a non-problem look like one.

## Verifying the message was genuine

A changed sender pattern is also what phishing looks like, so the check was worth doing. It is
**confirmed legitimate**, and the decisive evidence is not the email itself:

- the **SNAPP portal**, accessed by logging in directly, shows *"With editor — action needed"* for
  submission `35174617-3240-44d6-bb1c-fcc74ec1cd4f`;
- the editor's name, the support contact on the `@springernature.com` domain, and the submission
  version (v.1.1) all match between portal and email.

A forged email cannot alter what an authenticated portal session displays. **General rule for future
correspondence: act on what the portal shows, never on an email's links or attachments alone.**
